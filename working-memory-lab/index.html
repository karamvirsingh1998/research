Why short-term memory slips, and what actually helps

Short-term (working) memory holds about four chunks for a few seconds while prefrontal neurons keep firing. Each experiment below lets you test or simulate one way it fails.

Memory self-check with a scored report

Five short tasks used in clinical memory testing, plus a questionnaire about everyday lapses. Your scores are compared with healthy adults of your age. Do it in a quiet place, without writing anything down.

    Experiment 1 · Capacity

    Test your digit span

    Digits flash one at a time. Type them back in order. Each correct answer adds a digit. Most adults top out around 7 single digits, which is roughly 4 chunks.

    Experiment 3 · Neural drift

    Watch a memory drift in the prefrontal cortex

    A ring of neurons holds a remembered direction as a "bump" of activity. Random noise nudges the bump while you wait, so the memory gets less precise rather than vanishing. This is the bump-attractor account from Wimmer et al. (2014).

    Seconds held0.0
    Recall error–

    Press Present a cue. The amber tick is the true direction; it hides after one second. Wait, then press Recall now.

    Noise levels map loosely to tiredness, stress, and distraction. Longer waits plus more noise means bigger errors.

    Experiment 4 · Evidence

    What improves it, by effect size

    Standardized mean difference (SMD) from meta-analyses of randomized trials. 0.2 is small, 0.5 is moderate. Grey bars mean the benefit is weak, inconsistent, or does not carry over to daily life.

    Exercise: Zhidong et al. 2021 (older adults, 45–60 min, 3×/week, 6+ months). Brain training: Syed et al. 2024 second-order meta-analysis in adults 18–55. Mindfulness: Yakobi et al. 2021 found no working-memory gain. Sleep is not shown as an SMD but its loss reliably impairs memory, and exercise appears to work partly through better sleep.

    Educational simulation, not a diagnostic tool. New or worsening forgetfulness, confusion, or trouble with daily tasks is worth checking with a doctor; causes like B12 or thyroid problems are treatable.
